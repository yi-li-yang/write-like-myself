# Writing for a reader

## What writing is

Writing moves an idea from one mind to another through words alone. The writer is absent when the reader reads. The reader cannot ask what a word means, why one step follows another or what the point is. So everything the reader needs must already be on the page.

This one fact explains the others. The reader knows only what the page says and what they bring to it. They take in the words one at a time and in the order given, and they can hold only a little at once. At every sentence they decide whether to keep reading and whether to believe.

It also explains where good writing starts. A writer cannot put on the page a thought they have not finished. Most unclear writing is unclear thinking. So the work begins before the first sentence. It begins with knowing what this reader should understand.

## What good writing asks of the writer

Each demand of good writing is the writer doing the reader's work in advance.

Before writing, the writer settles two things. The first is a point that fits in a sentence or two. The second is who the reader is, what they already know and why they are reading. The text can then tell one story. It starts from something the reader already accepts, shows a question that matters to them, and ends with the answer. A paper, a proposal and an email all follow this order at different lengths.

While writing, the writer gives the ideas in the order the reader needs them. Each part tells the reader early what it is for. Each sentence starts from something the reader already has and ends with what is new, because readers give the most weight to the end of a sentence. Each sentence carries one complete thought, and its form shows how that thought relates to the one before. Ideas that are parallel take parallel form.

Words are concrete and specific, because a reader thinks in particular things and must translate every abstract word back into them. Each word keeps one meaning, and each thing keeps one name throughout.

The writer keeps what was observed apart from what it is taken to mean, so that the reader can judge the step between them. Each claim is exactly as strong as its evidence, and each choice comes with its reason.

Language must be precise first, then clear, then brief. Brevity never comes at the cost of precision or clarity. Within that limit, the writer removes every word that does no work for the reader.

The writer aims to inform the reader and never to impress them, and speaks to them as one person to another.

After writing, the writer reads the text as the reader would. Wherever the reader would stumble, the writer goes back to the thought and recasts the sentence. Swapping one word for another rarely helps, because the fault is usually in the thinking. For an important text, the writer also asks a reader with no background what each paragraph told them. The writer cannot do this alone, because the writer already knows what the text means.

## Why AI writing is noticed

A language model can produce text that looks like writing without a point to make or a reader to reach. It reaches for ready-made phrases that save it the work of thinking. The text then has the surface of writing but no purpose behind it, and readers sense this. The signs are familiar. Sentences stop to explain, claims are hedged and inflated at once, and points are arranged for their sound. These habits are only symptoms. They come from writing without knowing what to say to whom. A model that knows its point and its reader has no use for them. So this skill does not list habits to avoid. It asks the model to think first and to write for one reader. The habits change from one model to the next, but this cause does not.

## Sources

Four works give the same account at greater length.

- David Lindsay, *Scientific Writing = Thinking in Words*. Writing is part of the thinking, and language must be precise, clear and brief, in that order.
- Joshua Schimel, *Writing Science*. A text tells a story. It moves from what the reader already knows to a challenge, then to the action taken and its resolution.
- William Strunk Jr, *The Elements of Style*. Every rule serves the reader, and every word must do work.
- ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English. Each word keeps one meaning, and short, complete sentences prevent misreading.
